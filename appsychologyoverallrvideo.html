Key Takeaways

• Psychology is the scientific study of behavior and mental processes.
• Behavior is influenced by biological, cognitive, developmental, and social factors.
• The brain and nervous system work together to regulate thoughts, emotions, and actions.
• Memory involves encoding, storage, and retrieval of information.
• Learning occurs through classical conditioning, operant conditioning, and observation.
• Human development is shaped by both genetics (nature) and environment (nurture).
• Personality and social influences explain individual differences in behavior.
• Psychological disorders can be understood and treated using evidence-based approaches.
• Psychologists use scientific methods to study behavior and evaluate research findings.

Most Important Brain Structures

Frontal Lobe — Decision making, planning, personality, voluntary movement.
Parietal Lobe — Touch and spatial awareness.
Temporal Lobe — Hearing, language, memory.
Occipital Lobe — Vision.
Amygdala — Fear and emotion.
Hippocampus — Formation of new memories.
Hypothalamus — Homeostasis, hunger, thirst, hormones.
Cerebellum — Balance and coordination.
Brainstem — Breathing, heartbeat, survival functions.

Most Important Neurotransmitters

Dopamine — Reward, motivation, movement.
Serotonin — Mood, sleep, appetite.
Acetylcholine — Muscle movement, learning, memory.
Norepinephrine — Alertness, stress response.
GABA — Main inhibitory neurotransmitter; calms neural activity.
Glutamate — Main excitatory neurotransmitter; learning and memory.

Most Important Psychologists

Wilhelm Wundt — First psychology laboratory; structuralism.
William James — Functionalism.
Sigmund Freud — Psychoanalytic theory.
Ivan Pavlov — Classical conditioning.
John B. Watson — Behaviorism.
B.F. Skinner — Operant conditioning.
Albert Bandura — Observational learning (Bobo Doll Experiment).
Jean Piaget — Cognitive development.
Mary Ainsworth — Attachment theory (Strange Situation).
Abraham Maslow — Hierarchy of Needs.
Carl Rogers — Humanistic psychology.

Most Important Experiments

Pavlov's Dogs — Classical conditioning.
Little Albert (Watson) — Conditioned fear.
Skinner Box — Operant conditioning.
Bandura's Bobo Doll Experiment — Observational learning.
Asch Line Study — Conformity.
Milgram Obedience Experiment — Obedience to authority.
Stanford Prison Experiment (Zimbardo) — Social roles and behavior.
Harlow's Monkey Experiment — Attachment and comfort.
Ainsworth's Strange Situation — Attachment styles.

AP Exam Focus

• Apply psychological concepts to real-life scenarios.
• Distinguish similar psychological theories and terminology.
• Analyze research studies and experimental design.
• Interpret graphs and research findings.
• Understand famous psychologists and landmark experiments.
• Explain how biological, cognitive, developmental, and social factors interact to influence behavior.
