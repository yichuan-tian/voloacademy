Key Takeaways

• Life is built upon four major biological macromolecules: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.
• Cell structure determines function, and cells maintain homeostasis through selective transport across membranes.
• Energy is transformed through photosynthesis and cellular respiration, with ATP serving as the cell's primary energy currency.
• Cells communicate through signaling pathways and reproduce through mitosis and meiosis.
• Heredity is governed by Mendelian genetics, chromosome behavior during meiosis, and patterns of inheritance.
• The Central Dogma describes the flow of genetic information:
    DNA → RNA → Protein
• Evolution occurs through natural selection, driven by variation, mutation, genetic drift, and gene flow.
• Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium provides a model for populations that are not evolving.
• Ecosystems transfer energy through trophic levels while cycling matter through the carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and water cycles.
• Biological systems are interconnected, and understanding relationships between structure, function, energy, information, and evolution is essential for success on the AP Biology exam.

Must Know Equations

• Photosynthesis:
6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

• Cellular Respiration:
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP

• Hardy-Weinberg Allele Frequency:
p + q = 1

• Hardy-Weinberg Genotype Frequency:
p² + 2pq + q² = 1

AP Exam Focus

• Explain how biological structure relates to function.
• Analyze experimental data, graphs, and scientific investigations.
• Apply concepts across multiple units instead of memorizing isolated facts.
• Interpret unfamiliar biological scenarios using core principles.
• Support answers with scientific evidence and biological reasoning rather than memorization alone.
