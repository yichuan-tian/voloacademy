Key Takeaways

• Limits describe function behavior and establish the foundation of calculus.
• Derivatives measure instantaneous rates of change and are used to analyze functions.
• Integrals represent accumulation, area, volume, and total change.
• The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects differentiation and integration.
• Differential equations model changing systems and predict future behavior.
• Parametric, polar, and vector-valued functions extend calculus beyond Cartesian coordinates.
• Infinite sequences and series approximate functions and model infinite processes.
• Taylor and Maclaurin series are powerful polynomial approximations for transcendental functions.
• Calculus BC emphasizes connecting multiple representations—graphs, equations, tables, and words—to solve complex problems.
